## Legal & Regulatory Anchors

| Instrument | Artikel | Binding | Jurisdictie | Toelichting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AMLR Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 | Art. 33–36 | Hard law | EU | Requires institutions to investigate and, if necessary, report suspicious transactions and to establish internal escalation procedures |
| FATF Recommendation 20 | Rec. 20 | Guidance | Global | Emphasises that suspicious transactions should be evaluated by staff with expertise and escalated appropriately before reporting |
| EBA Guidelines on ML/TF Risk Factors | Sec. 4.4 | Supervisory | EU | Provides guidance on triggers for escalation and the need for corroborating evidence and human judgment |

## Normative Statement

Escalation of crypto compliance cases **MUST** be driven by documented indicators, contextual analysis and corroborating evidence, and must include human review prior to deciding whether to file suspicious transaction reports or take other actions. Institutions **MUST NOT** rely solely on automated systems or rigid rules for escalation decisions. The rationale for escalation or non‑escalation **MUST** be recorded.

## Operational Layer

* Define clear escalation criteria, including specific red flags, risk thresholds and patterns that require further investigation.
* Ensure that alerts generated by monitoring systems are reviewed by analysts who assess context, gather additional information and decide whether the case meets escalation criteria.
* Provide investigators with access to relevant data sources (on‑chain analytics, KYC records, OSINT) and tools to document their analysis and conclusions.
* Escalated cases are presented to a senior compliance review committee or designated officers for final determination and, if necessary, STR submission.

## Decision Logic

1. **If** an alert meets predefined escalation criteria and corroborating evidence confirms potential illicit activity **→** elevate the case for senior review and potential STR filing.
2. **If** evidence is ambiguous or incomplete but risk factors persist **→** keep the case under investigation, gather more data and re‑evaluate at set intervals.
3. **If** a case is determined to be a false positive after analysis **→** document the rationale for closure and any lessons learned to improve monitoring rules.

## Operational Controls

**Control objective:** Ensure that cases are escalated appropriately based on evidence and human judgment, leading to consistent and defensible decisions.

**Control description:** A case management workflow enforces documentation of analysis and decision points. Escalation thresholds are defined and regularly reviewed. High‑impact decisions require approval from the compliance committee. Training and quality reviews ensure analysts apply criteria consistently.

**Control owner:** Compliance investigations team and review committee.

**Review frequency:** Continuous for individual cases; escalation criteria reviewed quarterly; overall process audited annually.

**Dependencies:** Effective monitoring and alerting systems, access to data, trained analysts and review committee availability.

## Evidence & Audit Artifacts

* Case files containing alerts, analysis notes, corroborating evidence and escalation decisions.
* Records of review committee meetings and final determinations, including reasons for escalation or closure.
* Logs of false positives and feedback to monitoring rule calibration.
* Documentation of training provided to analysts on escalation criteria and procedures.

## Edge Cases

1. Multiple alerts across different customers pointing to coordinated illicit activity; escalation may involve combining cases and addressing systemic risk rather than individual customers.
2. Cases involving privileged customers (e.g., politically exposed persons); escalation procedures must ensure independence and avoid undue influence.
3. New patterns of illicit behaviour not captured by existing escalation criteria; analysts must exercise judgement and propose updates to criteria.

## Anti‑Patterns

1. Automatically escalating every alert that crosses a threshold without contextual analysis, overwhelming senior reviewers and reducing effectiveness.
2. Failing to document the reasons for not escalating a case, leaving gaps that cannot be justified to regulators or auditors.

## AI Usage Constraints

AI tools can prioritise alerts and suggest escalation based on patterns, but decisions must be vetted by humans. AI recommendations should be transparent, and analysts must understand why a case is flagged. AI cannot substitute for documented human analysis and decision‑making.
