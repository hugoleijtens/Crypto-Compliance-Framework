## Legal & Regulatory Anchors

| Instrument | Artikel | Binding | Jurisdictie | Toelichting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| AMLR Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 | Art. 19 & 33 | Hard law | EU | Permits termination of customer relationships when AML/CFT obligations cannot be fulfilled; requires proportionality and documentation |
| FATF Recommendation 11 & 26 | Rec. 11 & 26 | Guidance | Global | Advises institutions to consider exiting relationships where risks cannot be mitigated, while emphasising proportionate action and avoiding wholesale de‑risking |
| EBA Guidelines on ML/TF Risk Factors | Sec. 7 | Supervisory | EU | Stipulates that exit or de‑risking decisions should be evidence‑based, duly justified and documented |

## Normative Statement

Exit and de‑risking decisions regarding crypto customers or counterparties **MUST** be evidence‑based, proportionate and documented. Institutions **MUST** document the specific risks and control failures leading to termination, obtain appropriate approvals and consider whether alternative risk mitigation measures exist. Mass de‑risking without individual assessment **MUST NOT** occur.

## Operational Layer

* Establish criteria and procedures for terminating or restricting relationships, including minimum evidence thresholds (e.g., repeated unexplained suspicious activity, failure to provide required documentation, links to illicit actors).
* Document the decision process, including risk assessments, controls attempted, evidence collected and rationale for concluding that risk cannot be mitigated.
* Require approvals from senior compliance and legal management before executing de‑risking actions. For high‑impact cases, involve an escalation committee or board.
* Communicate the decision to the customer or counterparty as appropriate, without disclosing sensitive information (e.g., suspicion thresholds). Maintain records of communication and any responses.

## Decision Logic

1. **If** documented evidence shows persistent high‑risk behaviour or inability to satisfy due‑diligence requirements despite remediation efforts **→** recommend exit or restrictions and obtain necessary approvals.
2. **If** risk can be mitigated through enhanced monitoring, transaction limits or additional conditions **→** apply these measures and monitor before considering termination.
3. **If** the relationship is terminated due to sanctions or legal prohibitions **→** follow regulatory reporting requirements and freeze or return assets as required by law.

## Operational Controls

**Control objective:** Ensure exit and de‑risking decisions are made based on evidence, documented rationale and appropriate approval levels.

**Control description:** A de‑risking procedure requires completion of a termination assessment form capturing evidence, attempted mitigation measures and legal advice. Approvals are tracked electronically. Decision outcomes and communications are recorded in the case management system. Regular reviews ensure consistency and proportionality across cases.

**Control owner:** Compliance risk management team, with involvement of legal and senior management.

**Review frequency:** Case‑by‑case; overall de‑risking policy reviewed annually or in response to regulatory guidance.

**Dependencies:** Comprehensive risk assessments, evidence gathering capabilities, escalation procedures and governance oversight.

## Evidence & Audit Artifacts

* Termination assessment forms and supporting documentation (e.g., transaction analysis, due‑diligence notes, regulatory correspondence).
* Records of approvals by compliance and legal management or escalation committees.
* Communications to customers or counterparties regarding termination or restrictions.
* Policy and procedural documentation governing de‑risking and exit decisions.

## Edge Cases

1. Customers contesting termination decisions; institutions must provide evidence of compliance with regulations and fairness while maintaining confidentiality of suspicious activity reports.
2. Legal or regulatory mandates requiring immediate de‑risking (e.g., sanctions listing) may shorten the normal decision process; documentation should still capture rationale and steps taken.
3. Termination of relationships with systemic or critical service providers (e.g., payment processors) may have operational impacts; careful risk transfer planning is required.

## Anti‑Patterns

1. Instituting blanket bans on entire customer segments (e.g., all crypto businesses) without individual risk assessment, leading to regulatory criticism and financial exclusion.
2. Failing to document the specific reasons for exiting a relationship, making it difficult to defend decisions to regulators or to learn from past cases.

## AI Usage Constraints

AI tools may assist in identifying high‑risk patterns and recommending de‑risking candidates, but final exit decisions must be made by senior compliance and legal professionals. AI models cannot independently authorise de‑risking actions.
